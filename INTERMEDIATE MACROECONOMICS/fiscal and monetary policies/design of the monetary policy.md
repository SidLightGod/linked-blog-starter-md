# Money growth targets and target ranges 
1. the central bank chose a target rate for nominal money growth corresponding to the inflation rate it wanted to achieve in medium run. 
2. in the short run, the central bank allowed for deviations of nominal money growth from the target
3. to communicate to the public both it wanted to achieve in medium run and what it intended to do in the short run, the central bank announced a range for the rate of nominal money growth it intended to achieve 

# Inflation targeting
in most countries the central bank have defined as their primary goal the achievement of a low inflation rate, both in the short run and the medium run. This is known as inflation targeting 
1. trying to achieve an inflation target in the medium run would seem, and indeed is, a clear improvement over trying to achieve a nominal money growth target. After all, in the medium run, the primary goal of monetary policy is to achieve a given rate of inflation. Better to have an inflation rate as the target than nominal money growth target. 
2. trying to achieve a given inflation target in the short run would seem controversial. Focusing exclusively on the inflation rate would seem to eliminate any role monetary policy could play in reducing output fluctuations. But in fact this is not necessarily the case 

let us revisit the Phillips curve 
$$ \pi_t = \pi_{t-1} - \alpha(u_t - u_n) $$
letting the inflation target be $\pi^*$ and suppose the central bank could achieve this target exactly in every period then the relation would become 
$$ \pi^* = \pi^* - \alpha(u_t-u_n) $$ 
the unemployment rate would always equal the natural rate and therefore it implies that the output would equal the natural level. 

> as an effect it would lead the central bank to act in such a way as to eliminate all deviations of output from its natural level. 

> the intuition is that if the central bank saw that an adverse demand shock was going to lead to a recession, it would know that the inflation would go below the target level, in absence of monetary expansion. To maintain stable inflation the central bank would rely on monetary expansion to avoid recession. **THE CONVERSE WOULD APPLY IN CASE OF FAVORABLE DEMAND SHOCK. FEARING AN INCREASE IN INFLATION BEYOND THE TARGET, THE CENTRAL BANK WOULD RELY ON MONETARY CONTRACTION TO SLOW THE ECONOMY AND KEEP OUTPUT AT THE NATURAL LEVEL OF OUTPUT.**



# Interest Rate Rules 
Taylor rules goes as follows 
1. let $\pi_t$ be the rate of inflation and $\pi^*$ be the target rate of inflation 
2. let $i_t$ be the nominal rate of interest controlled by the central bank and $i^*$ be the target nominal interest rate 
3. let $u_t$ be the unemployment rate and $u_n$ be the natural unemployment rate 

he argued that the central bank should use the following rule $$ i_t = i^* + a(\pi_t - \pi^*) - b(u_t - u_n) $$
here a and b are positive coefficients 

##### important conclusions which follows from the Taylor Rule 
1. if the inflation is equal to the target rate of inflation and the unemployment rate is equal to the natural rate of unemployment, then the bank should set the nominal interest rate equal to the target value 
2. if the inflation is higher than the target level, the central bank should increase the nominal interest rate above the target rate, this would increase the unemployment and thus drive the inflation rate down. 
3. Taylor pointed out that the inflation coefficient a should be larger than 1, because for spending the real interest rate matters and not the nominal interest rate. in other words the central bank should increase the nominal interest rate more than one for one with the inflation. 
4. if the unemployment is higher than the natural rate of unemployment then the central bank should decrease the nominal rate of interest, this would lead to an increase in the output and therefore a decrease in the unemployment rate, the higher the b value, the more the central bank will be willing to deviate from the inflation target to keep the unemployment close to the natural rate of unemployment 

> Taylor did not argue that it should be followed blindly. Many other events such as the exchange rate crisis, need to change the composition of spending on goods, and thus the mix between monetary policy and fiscal policy
