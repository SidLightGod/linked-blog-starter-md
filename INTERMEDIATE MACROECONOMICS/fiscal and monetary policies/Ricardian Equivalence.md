how does taking into account the budget constraint affect the way we should think of the effects of deficit on the output? 
1. one extreme view is that once the government budget constraint is taken into the consideration, neither the deficit nor the debt has an effect on the economic activities
2. this argument is known as the Ricardian Equivalence proposition. 

##### let us analyze the logic behind it using the example of tax changes
1. suppose the government announces that it would be cutting the taxes by 1 and as it does so, to repay the debt, it will increase the taxes by (1+r) next year. 
2. our point of discussion is what the effect will be of the initial tax cut on consumption 
3. one answer is no effect at all, because the consumers realize that the tax cut is not much of a gift because the tax cut this year will be exactly offset by tax increase the following year. 
4. in other words their after tax labor income stays unaffected. the net effect of two changes is exactly zero. 
5. there is another way of coming to the same conclusion but instead of looking at the consumption we focus on the saving. this implies that, saying consumers do not change their consumption following a tax cut means that the private saving increases one for one with deficit. 

> so the Ricardian Equivalence proposition states that if the government finances a given path of spending through deficits, private saving will increase one for one with decrease in public saving, leaving total saving unchanged. The total amount left for investment will not be affected. **Over time the mechanics of government budget constraint implies that the government debt will increase but this increase will not come at the expense of capital accumulation.**

#### some important things to keep in mind 
1. But insofar the future tax increases appear distant and more uncertain, the consumer are in fact more likely to ignore them. 
2. this maybe the case because they expect to die before taxes go up, or, more likely because they do not think that far into the future. In either case the Ricardian equivalence is likely to fail. 
3. so it is safe to conclude that the budget deficits have an important effect on the activity, although a smaller one than thought of before the Ricardian Equivalence. 
4. in short run, large deficits are likely to results in higher demand and to higher output. In long run, higher government debts lower the capital accumulation and as a result lowers output.
