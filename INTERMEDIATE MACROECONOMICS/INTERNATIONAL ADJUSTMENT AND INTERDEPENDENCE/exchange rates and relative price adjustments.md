###### sticky real wages(wages that are difficult to change) and real disturbances ?
1. suppose our export demand declines permanently because of say introduction of superior technology abroad. 
2. to return to the full employment, the relative prices of our goods fall in order to encourage foreign demand. 
3. the relative price can fall if we devalue, and workers succeed in restoring their real wages and prices are marked up on wages, there will be no change in the relative price of our goods. Then the only way of reducing the real wage would be protracted unemployment. 
4. the question of importance henceforth is of how flexible the real wages are and to an extent that is a question of institutional arrangements. 
5. in small, open economies with substantial cost of living indexation in wage agreements, it may indeed by very difficult to change real wages and relative prices through exchange rate changes. 
6. in general the countries that devalue have to use restrictive aggregate demand policies to make sure that the induced increases in prices do not simply undo the real effects of nominal devaluation.
