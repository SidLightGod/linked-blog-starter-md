1. because of their side effects, policies to restore external balance be combined with policies to achieve full employment. 
2. policies to create employment will typically worsen the external balance and the policies to create trade surplus will affect employment 
3. in general it is necessary to combine expenditure switching policies, which shifts demand between domestic and imported goods, and expenditure reducing policies in order to cope with two targets of internal and external balance. 
4. this point is of general importance and continues to apply when we take into account capital flows and other phenomenon omitted in this section. 
5. **one method of adjusting a current account deficit is to impose tariffs - taxes on imports**
6. however tariffs can not be used freely to adjust the balance of trade, partly because international organizations and agreements such as GATT and IMF outlaw or at least frown on, the use of tariffs. 
7. another way of adjusting the current account deficit is to use policies to reduce the aggregate demand, these are expenditure reducing policies. 
8. we know that NX = Y - (C + I + G), thus a balance of trade can be achieved by reducing the spending relative to the income. The line between external deficit and budget deficit is NX = S - I + T - G
9. here S denotes the private saving and T - G denotes the budget surplus. the above equation shows an immediate relationship between budget and the external balance. 
10. if saving and investment were constant the changes in the budget would translate one for one into changes in the external balance.
