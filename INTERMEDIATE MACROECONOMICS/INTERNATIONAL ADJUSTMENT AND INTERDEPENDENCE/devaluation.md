1. the unemployment that typically accompanies the automatic adjustment and desirability of free trade, which argues against the use of tariffs, both suggest the need for an alternative policy for restoring internal and external balance.
2. the major policy for dealing with the payments deficit is devaluation, which is usually combined with restrictive monetary and fiscal policy. 
3. a devaluation is the increase in the domestic currency price of foreign exchange. Given the nominal prices in the two countries, devaluation increases the relative price of the imported goods in the devaluing country and reduces the relative price of the exports from the devaluing country.
4. devaluation is primarily an expenditure shifting policy. 

###### how does devaluation work?

2. consider first the special case of a country that has been in full employment with the balance of trade equilibrium and is at the point E.
3. now let their be an exogenous decline in export earnings so that the NX = 0 schedule shifts to the left to NX' = 0. 
4. with a lower demand for exports and with a fixed exchange rate, output would decline. The AD schedule moves to the left as a result of the fall in exports. 
5. the lower level of income reduces imports, but not enough to make up for the loss of export revenue. The net effects are therefore unemployment and a trade deficit. 
6. the automatic adjustment mechanism would work, but it would work slowly to restore the equilibrium. Alternatively the country can devalue its currency.
7. this has the obvious advantage of not requiring a protracted recession to reduce domestic costs. The adjustment is done by a stroke of a pen - a devaluation of the currency. 

###### why would devaluation achieve the adjustment? 
1. given the price of the foreign goods in terms of foreign currency, a devaluation raises the price of the relative price of the foreign goods. Imports fall, and exports rise. 
2. the economy was in the balance of trade equilibrium at full employment. The disturbance to the economy took place in the trade account.
3. if we could move the NX' = 0 locus back to the full employment level of income - as we could with a devaluation - both internal and external balance would be attained 
4. **put generally a country can not secure both external and internal balance following a disturbance by using just one instrument of policy.**
5. ***a general rule of policy making is that we need to use as many policy instruments as we have policy targets***
