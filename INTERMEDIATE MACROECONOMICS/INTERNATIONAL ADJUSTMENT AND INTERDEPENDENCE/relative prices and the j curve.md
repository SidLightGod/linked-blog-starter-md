###### the effect of change in the relative prices on the trade balance and the possibility that a depreciation worsens the trade balance? 
1. we write our trade balance, measured in the terms of domestic goods, as $$NX = X - \frac{eP_f}{P}Q$$
2. where X denotes the foreign demand for our goods or exports and Q denotes our own import quantity. The term $\frac{eP_f}{P}Q$ thus measures the value of our imports in terms of domestic goods. 
3. suppose that we now have an exchange depreciation and that is the first instance, domestic and foreign prices, P and Pf, are unchanged. Then the relative price of our imports ePf/P, rises. 

###### the two fold effect of this? 
1. first if the physical volume of the imports does not change, their value measured in domestic currency unambiguously increase because of the higher price. 
2. this means higher import spending and thus a worsening of the trade balance. This the source of potentially perverse response of the trade balance to exchange depreciation. 
3. however there are two volume responses that run in the opposite direction. Exports should rise because our goods are now cheaper for foreigner to buy and volume of the imports should decline because imports are more expensive.
4. exports should rise because our goods are cheaper for foreigners to buy and the volume of the imports should decline because imports are more expensive
5. the question is therefore whether the volume effects on imports and exports outweigh the price effect, that is depreciation raises or lowers net exports. 
6. the short term volume effect say within a year are quite small and thus do not outweigh the price effect. The long term effects, by contrast are substantial and certainly enough to make the trade balance respond in the normal fashion to a relative price change. 
   

###### why does this pattern responses take place? 
1. first low short term and high long term volume effects result from the time consumers and producers take to adjust to changes in the relative prices.
2. some of these changes may be instantaneous, but it is clear that tourism patterns, for example, may take 6 months to a year to adjust and the reallocation of the production internationally in response to changes in relative cost and prices may take years. 

###### why do these lag imply about the impact of relative prices changes on the trade balance? 
1. suppose that at a particular time starting with a deficit we have a depreciation that raises the relative price of imports. 
2. the short term effects result primarily from increased import prices with very few offsetting volume effects. Therefore the trade balance initially worsens. 
3. over time, as trade balance adjusts to the changed relative prices, exports rise and the import volume progressively declines. The volume effects come to dominate, and in the long run, the trade balance shows an improvement. 
4. this pattern of adjustment is referred to as the J curve effect, because diagrammatically the response of the trade balance looks like a J. 
5. The medium term problem of sticky real wages and the J curve effect provides important clues for the interpretation of macroeconomic experiences across countries, particularly in showing why depreciations typically do not lead to improvements in the current account in the short term.
