1. the most common type of foreign exchange transaction involves payment and receipt of the foreign exchange within 2 business days after the day the transaction is agreed upon. 
2. the two day period gives adequate time to the parties to send instructions to the debit and credit the appropriate bank accounts at home and abroad. This type of transaction is called spot transaction, and the exchange rate at which the transaction takes place is called the spot rate. 
3. besides spot transactions there are forward transactions. a forward transaction involves an agreement today to buy or sell a specified amount of foreign currency at a specified future date and at rate agreed upon today (forward rate.)
4. the typical forward contract is for 1 month, three month or six months. three months is the most common though. forward contracts for longer periods are not very common because of the great uncertainties involved. 
5. however the forward contracts can be renegotiated for one or more periods when they become due. 
6. the equilibrium forward rate is determined by the intersection of the market demand and market supply of foreign exchange for future delivery 
7. the demand and supply for the foreign currency arise in the course of hedging from the foreign exchange speculations and from covered interest arbitrage. 
8. if the **forward rate is below the present spot rate, the foreign currency is said to be at a forward discount.** 
9. on the other hand if the forward rate is above the present spot rate, the foreign currency is said to be at **forward premium**. 
10. $$FD \space or \space FP = \frac{FR-SR}{SR} * 4 * 100$$
11. where FR is the forward rate and SR is the spot rate, the multiplication by 4 is to express the FD- or FP+ on a yearly basis, and multiplication by 100 is to express the FD or FP in percentages
