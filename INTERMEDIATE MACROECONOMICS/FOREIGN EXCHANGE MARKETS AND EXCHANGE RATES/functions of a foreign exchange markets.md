1. by far the principle function of the exchange markets is the transfer of the funds or purchasing power from one nation and currency to another. 
2. this is usually done by an electric transfer and increasingly through the internet
3. with it a domestic bank instructs a correspondent bank in a foreign monetary center to pay a specified amount of the local currency to a person, firm, or account. 
4. the nation's central bank acts as a clearing houses for the foreign exchange demanded and supplied in the course of foreign transactions by nation's residents 
5. if the country's total foreign exchange demand in the course of its foreign transactions exceeds its total foreign exchange earnings, the rate at which the currencies exchange for one another will have to change to equilibrate the total quantities demanded and supplied. 
6. if such adjustments in the foreign exchange were not allowed, the nation's commercial bank would have to borrow from nation's central bank
7. the central bank would then act as the lender of last resorts and draw down its foreign exchange reserves. 
8. on the other hand if the nation generated an excess of foreign exchange during its course of business transactions with other nations, this excess supply would be exchanged for the national currency at the nation's central bank, thus increasing the nation's foreign currency reserves. 
9. thus four levels of transactors or participators can be identified in foreign exchange markets
10. at the bottom, or at the last level, are such traditional users such as tourists, importers, exporters, investors, and so on. 
11. at the next, or the second level are the commercial banks which act as the clearinghouses between the users and the earners of foreign exchange. 
12. at the third level are the foreign exchange brokers, through whom the nation's commercial banks even out their foreign exchange inflows and outflows among themselves. 
13. finally at the fourth and the highest level is the nation's central bank which acts as the seller and buyer of the last resort when nation's total foreign exchange earnings and expenditures are unequal.
14. another function of the foreign exchange market is the credit function. the credit is usually needed when the goods are in transit and also to allow the buyer time to resell the goods and make the payment.
