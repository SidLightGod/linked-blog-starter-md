1. hedging refers to the avoidance of a foreign exchange risk, or the covering of an exchange position. 
2. the cost of avoiding foreign exchange risks this way is once again equal to the positive difference between borrowing and deposit rates of interest. 
3. covering the foreign exchange risk in the spot market however has a very serious disadvantage. The businessperson or investor must borrow or tie up his or her own funds for three months. 
4. to avoid this, hedging usually takes place in the forward market, where no borrowing or tying up of funds is required. 
5. thus the importer could buy the said currency forward for delivery/and payment in three months at today's three month forward rate. 
6. a foreign exchange risk can be hedged and an open position avoided in the futures or options market. 
7. in a world of foreign exchange uncertainty, the ability of the trader and investors to hedge greatly facilitates the international flow of trade and investment. 
8. without hedging there would be smaller international capital flows, less trade and specialization in production, and smaller benefits from trade
