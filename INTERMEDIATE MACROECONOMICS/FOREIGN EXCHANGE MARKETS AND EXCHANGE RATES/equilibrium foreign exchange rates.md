1. the exchange rate between the dollars and euros is the number of dollars needed to purchase one euro. 
2. which means $R = \frac{dollar}{euro}$ so if R = 1, then it implies that 1 dollar is required to purchase 1 pound
3. under a flexible rate of exchange the dollar price of euro is determined using the forces of demand and supply just like any other commodity

5. the vertical axis measures the exchange rate and the horizontal axis measures the quantity of euros. The market supply and demand curve for euros intersect at point E, defining the equilibrium rate of exchange. 
6. at a higher exchange rate the quantity of euros supplies exceeds the quantity demanded and the exchange rate falls back to the equilibrium at 1. 
7. at an exchange rate below 1, the quantity of euros demanded exceeds the quantity of it supplied and the exchange rate bids up to R = 1. 
8. the US demand curve for euros is negatively inclined, implying that lower the exchange rate higher the demand for euros and vice versa. 
9. if the US demand curve shifted up and intersected the point G, the equilibrium rate would be 1.50 and the quantity of euros would be 300 million, the dollar would then said to be depreciated, as it now requires 1.50 dollars to purchase one euro. 
10. depreciation therefore refers to the increase in the domestic price of a foreign currency. 
11. on the other hand if the US demand curve shifted downwards and intersected, the exchange rate would fall to 0.5 and dollar is said to have been appreciated 
12. once the exchange rate for each a pair of currency with dollar is established, the exchange rate between two currencies themselves, ***cross exchange rate***, can easily be determined 
13. since over a time a currency can depreciate with respect to some currencies and appreciate against others, an effective exchange rate is calculated. this is the weighted average of the exchange rates between the currency and the nation's most important trade partners, with weights given on the basis of importance of nation's trade with each of these partners
