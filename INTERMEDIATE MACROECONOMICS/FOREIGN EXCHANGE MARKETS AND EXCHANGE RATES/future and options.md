1. a foreign exchange future is a forward contract for standardized currency amounts and a selected calendar dates traded on an organized market. 
2. the futures are different from the forward market in the sense that in the future market only a few currencies are traded, and the trade occurs in the standardized currency only, only for a few specific delivery dates, and are subject to daily limits on exchange rate fluctuations. 
3. futures contract can also be sold at any time up until maturity on an organized futures market, while forward contracts can not. 
4. **a foreign exchange option is a contract giving the purchaser the right, but not obligation, to buy (a call option), or to sell (a put action) a standard amount of a traded currency on a stated date, or at any time before a stated date.**
5. the buyer of the option has the choice to purchase or forego the purchase if it turns out to be unprofitable. 
6. the seller of the option, however must fulfill the contract if the buyer so desires. 
7. the buyer pays the seller a premium (the option price) ranging from 1 to 5 percent of contract's value for this privilege when he or she enters this contract. 
8. option are less flexible than forward contracts but in some cases they may be more useful.
