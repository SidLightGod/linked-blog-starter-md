The relation between inflation and unemployment can be explained as follows : $$ \pi = \pi^{e} - \alpha(u - u_n) $$
> this relationship explains how the inflation actually depends on the expected inflation and the difference between the actual unemployment and the natural rate of unemployment. The $\alpha$ captures the effect of unemployment on inflation, given expected inflation. 

#### why the FED can not actually achieve an unemployment rate below the natural level 
1. by accepting an inflation rate just above 1%, the FED can achieve an unemployment rate of 2% which is below the natural rate. 
2. however seeing the FED has increased the money growth more than it announced it would, wage setters are likely to smarten up and begin to expect positive inflation of 1%. If it still wishes to achieve an unemployment rate of 2%, it would have to accept an inflation rate of 2%. 
3. however if it does achieve 2% inflation, the wage setters are likely to increase their expectations of inflation further and so on. 
4. the eventual outcome is likely to be high inflation. Because the wage setters understand the FED's motives, the expected inflation catches up with actual inflation and FED will eventually be unsuccessful in its attempt to achieve an unemployment rate below the natural rate.
