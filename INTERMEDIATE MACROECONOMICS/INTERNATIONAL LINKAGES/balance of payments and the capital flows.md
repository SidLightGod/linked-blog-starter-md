###### some assumptions to be kept in mind 
1. the home country faces a given price of imports and a given export demand. 
2. we also assume that the world rate of interest is given. Moreover, with perfect capital mobility, capital flows into home country at an unlimited rate if our interest rate is above that abroad if our interest rate is above that abroad. Conversely, if our interest rate is below that abroad, capital outflows will be unlimited. 
3. Next we look at balance of payments. The balance of payments surplus, BP, is equal to the trade surplus, NX, plus the capital account surplus, CF : $$ BP = NX(Y, Y_f, R) + CF(i - i_j) $$
4. in the above equation the trade surplus is shown as a function of the domestic income and foreign income and the real exchange rate, and the capital account depending on the interest differential. 
5. An increase in the income worsens the trade balance, and an increase in the interest rate above the world level pulls in capital from abroad, and thus improves the capital account. 
6. It follows that when the income increases, even the tiniest increase in the interest rate is enough to maintain an overall balance of payments equilibrium. The trade deficit would be financed by a capital inflow.
