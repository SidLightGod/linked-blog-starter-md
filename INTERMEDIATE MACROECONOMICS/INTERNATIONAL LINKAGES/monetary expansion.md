3. in the above diagram we show the IS LM schedule as well as BP = 0 schedule, which now, because of the perfect capital mobility is a horizontal line. 
4. Only at a level of interest equal to those abroad $i = i_j$, can the country have payments balance. 
5. Ant any other interest rate, the capital flows are so massive that the balance of payment can not be in equilibrium, and the central bank has to intervene to maintain the exchange rate. This intervention shifts the LM schedule. 
6. consider specifically a monetary expansion that starts from point E. The LM schedule shifts down and to the right, and the economy moves from point E to point E'. But at point E' there is a large payment deficit and hence the pressure for the exchange rate to depreciate. 
7. the central bank must intervene, selling foreign money and receiving domestic money in exchange. The supply of domestic money hence declines. As a result, the LM schedule shifts back up and to the left. The process continues until the initial equilibrium at E is restored. 
8. indeed, with perfect capital mobility the economy never even gets to the point E'. The response of capital flows is so large and rapid that the central bank is forced to reverse the initial capital expansion of the money stock as soon as it attempts it. 
9. Conversely, any attempt to contract the money stock would immediately lead to vast reserve losses, forcing an expansion of money stock and return to the initial equilibrium.
