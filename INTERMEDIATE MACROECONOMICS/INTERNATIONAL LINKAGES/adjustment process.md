1. suppose then, that the increase in foreign demand takes place and that in response there is a tendency for output and income to increase. The induced increase in money demand will raise interest rates and thus bring us out of line with international interest rates. 
2. the resulting capital inflows immediately put pressure on the exchange rate. The capital inflow causes our currency to appreciate. 
3. the exchange appreciation means that the import prices fall and the domestic goods become relatively more expensive. Demand shifts away from the domestic goods, and net exports decline.

###### how far the exchange appreciation will go and to what to extent it will dampen the expansionary effect of increased net export? 
1. the exchange rate will keep appreciating as long as the interest rate exceeds the world level.
2. this implies that the exchange appreciation must continue until IS schedule has shifted back all the way to its initial position 
3. this adjustment is shown by the arrows along the LM curve, only when we return to point E will output and income have reached a level consistent with monetary expansion at the world rate of interest
4. **under the condition of perfect capital mobility, an expansion in exports have no long lasting effect on the equilibrium output.**
5. **with the perfect capital mobility the tendency of the interest rate to rise, as a result of the increase in the export demand, leads to currency appreciation and thus to a complete offset of the increase in exports.**
6. once we return to the point E, net exports are back to their initial level. The exchange rate has of course, appreciated. Imports will decrease as a consequences of the appreciation and the initial expansion in exports is, in part, offset by the appreciation of our exchange rate.
