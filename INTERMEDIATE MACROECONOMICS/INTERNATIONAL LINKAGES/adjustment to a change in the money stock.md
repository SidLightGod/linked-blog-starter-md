1. we start from an initial equilibrium point at E and consider an increase in the nominal quantity of the money, M bar
2. since the prices are given, we have an increase in the real money stock $\frac{\bar{M}}{\bar{P}}$, at E there will be an excess supply of real balances. To restore equilibrium, interest rates would have to be lower or income would have to be larger. 
3. Accordingly, the LM schedule shifts down and to the right LM'. 
4. we ask once again whether the equilibrium is in equilibrium at point E'. at E', goods and money markets are in equilibrium, but interest rates have fallen below the world level. 
5. capital outflows therefore put pressure on the exchange rate, leading to a deprecation. the exchange depreciation caused by the capital outflow lead to import prices to increase, domestic goods become more competitive, and the demand for our output expands 
6. the IS curve shifts out and to the right and it continues doing so until exchange depreciation has raised demand and output to the level indicated by point E''. Only at E" do we have goods and money market equilibrium compatible with the world rate of interest. 
7. consequently there is no tendency for the exchange rates and relative prices and hence demand to change.
