1. this increase in import demand caused by a one dollar increase in income is called the marginal propensity to import. 
2. the marginal propensity to import measures the fraction of an extra dollar of income spent on imports. 
3. the fact that the part of the income will be spent on the imports, implies that the IS curve will be steeper than it would be in a closed economy. 
4. for a given reduction in the interest rates it takes a smaller increase in output and income to restore goods market equilibrium. 
5. the open economy IS curve includes the net exports as a component of aggregate demand. Therefore, the level of competitiveness, as measured by the real exchange rate R, affects the IS curve 
6. a real depreciation increased the demand for domestic goods, shifting the IS curve to the right. Likewise, an increase in the foreign income and therefore an increase in the foreign spending on our goods will increase the exports or demand for our goods. Thus we write the IS curve : $$ Y = A(Y, i) + NX(Y, Y_f, R) $$
7. since the equilibrium level of income will now depend both on the foreign income and the real exchange rate, we will have to see how disturbances in foreign income, or real exchange rate changes, affect the equilibrium level of income.
