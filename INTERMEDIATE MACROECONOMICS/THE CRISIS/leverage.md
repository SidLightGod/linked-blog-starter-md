1. the capital ratio is defined as the ratio of the capital to the assets and is thus equal to 20%. 
2. its leverage ratio is defined as the ratio of assets to capital. 
3. now suppose some of the assets in each of the two banks go bad. the value of the assets of both banks, say drops from 100 to 90. 
4. the bank B now has an asset of 90 and liabilities of 80, and the capital of 90 - 80 = 10. The bank A now has an asset of 90 but liabilities of 95, and the capital of 90-95 = -5. 
5. Its liabilities exceed its assets and therefore it is bankrupt. 
6. **This is exactly what happened during the crisis, many banks had such high leverage ratios, that even limited losses in assets greatly increased the risk of bankruptcy.**

###### why did bank opt to take on more risk? 
1. the bank underestimated the risk they were taking, times were good, and, banks just like people, tend to underestimate the risk of bad times. 
2. second, the compensation of the bonus system also gave incentives to the managers to go for a high expected returns without fully taking into account the risk of bankruptcy into account. 
3. third while, financial regulation required bank to keep the capital ratio above the minimum, banks found new ways of avoiding the regulation, by creating new financial structures such as SIVs.
