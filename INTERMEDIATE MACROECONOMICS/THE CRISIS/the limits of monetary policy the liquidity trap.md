1. once people hold enough money for their transactional purposes, then they are indifferent between holding the rest of their financial wealth in the form of money or in the form of bonds. 
2. the reason is that both money and bonds pay the same interest rate, 0, thus the demand for money is shown in the graph above 
3. as the interest decreases, the people wish to hold more money, the demand for the money increases. 
4. as the interest decreases and become equal to zero, the people wish to hold money equal to at least the distance OB: this is what they need for the transaction purposes. But they are willing to hold even more money. therefore the demand for money becomes horizontal beyond point B. 

###### what happens when the supply of the money increases 
1. consider that the money supply is at $M^s$, so the interest rate is consistent with the financial markets equilibrium and is positive and equal to i. 
2. increasing the money supply from this point to $M^{s'}$ which leads to a decrease in the interest rate and the equilibrium is at B and when it shifts to $M^{s''}$ the equilibrium is at C in either case the interest rate is at 0 and an increase in the money supply has no impact on the interest rate. 

> in other words once the interest rate is equal to zero, the expansionary monetary policy becomes powerless, or in the words of Keynes, the increase in money falls into the liquidity trap. 


###### derive the LM curve 
![[Pasted image 20250405232323.png]]
1. Md shows the demand for money for a given level of income Y. the equilibrium at point A. this combination of income and interest rate gives the first point of the LM curve. 
2. the point A' shows the demand for money for a lower level of income and the corresponding interest rate which is at i' this combination gives the second point on the LM curve. 
3. for an even lower level of income where i = 0, the point A'' gives the equilibrium and we can plot this on the LM curve. 

> in the presence of liquidity trap the LM curve is given by the diagram above, where when the income is above Y'', it is upward sloping and for values of income less than Y'', the LM curve is flat and intuitively the interest rate just can not go below 0.
