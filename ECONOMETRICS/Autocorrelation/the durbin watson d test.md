1. the most celebrated test for detecting autocorrelation is that developed by Durbin and Watson, popularly known as the Durbin Watson d statistic. 
2. this is defined as the follow : ![[Pasted image 20250504170631.png]]
3. this is simply the ratio of the sum of squared differences in successive residuals to the RSS. It is to be noted that in the denominator the sample size is given as n - 1 because one observation is lost in taking successive differences. 
4. a great advantage of the d statistic is the simplicity; it is based on the OLS residuals which are routinely computed by most regression packages. It is now a common practice to report the Durbin Watson d along with summary statistics. 

###### what are the key assumptions behind the Durbin Watson statistic 
1. the regression model includes an intercept term. Therefore, it can not be used to determine autocorrelation in models of regression through the origin. 
2. the X variables are non stochastic, that is, their values are fixed in repeated sampling
3. the disturbances $u_t$ are generated by the following mechanism : $$ u_t = \rho u_{t-1} + v_t \space ; \space \space \space \space \space where,\space -1 \leq \rho \leq 1 $$
4. this states that the value of the disturbance term $u_t$, the disturbance term at time t, depends on its value in time period t - 1 and a purely random term $v_t$, And the extent of the dependence on the past value is measured by rho, this is also called the coefficient of the autocorrelation, which lies between -1 and 1. 
5. the mechanism mentioned above is also called the Markov first order regressive scheme or simply the first order autoregressive scheme usually denoted as the AR(1) scheme 

> the term autoregressive is appropriate because because the mechanism can be interpreted as the regression of ut on itself lagged in one period. And this is first order because ut and its immediate past value are involved; that is, the maximum lag is one time period. 

1. the regression does not contain the lagged values of the dependent variable as one of the explanatory variables. In other words, the test is not applicable to models such as $$ Y_t = B_1 + B_2X_t + B_3Y_{t-1} + u_t $$
2. models like the one above are called autoregressive models, a regression of a variable on itself with a lag as one of the explanatory variables.
