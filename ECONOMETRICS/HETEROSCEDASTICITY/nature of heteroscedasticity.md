1. the first figure shows as PDI increases, the mean, or the average, level of saving increases but the variance around its mean value remains the same at all levels of PDI. This is the case of homoscedasticity 
2. on the other hand figure b shows that although the average level of saving increases the variance of the saving does not remain same for all the levels of PDI. This is the case of heteroscedasticity 
3. researchers have found that the problem is heteroscedasticity is usually found in cross sectional data and not in time series data 
4. in cross sectional data we deal with members of a population at a given point of time, such as individual consumers or their families, firms, industries or geographical subdivisions such as state, country, or city. Moreover members may be of different sizes, in other words there may be some scale effect.
