1. even during the case of multicollinearity, as long as its not perfect collinearity, the OLS estimators still remain BLUE even though one or more of the partial regression coefficients in a multiple regression can be individually statistically significant. 
2. it is true that even in the case of near collinearity, the OLS estimators still remain unbiased. But it is to be kept in mind that the unbiasedness is a repeated sampling property, that if we kept the value of X fixed and obtain several samples, the average value of the estimates will tend to converge to the true population values of the estimate but this say nothing about the properties of the estimates given in any given sample. In reality we rarely have the luxury of replicating samples.
3. it is also true that the near collinearity does not destroy the minimum variance property of the OLS estimators. In the class of all linear unbiased estimators, OLS estimators have minimum variance. This does not however mean that the variance of an OLS estimator will be small in any given sample, in the sample at hand its variance is so large compared to the estimate that the computed t value is small, that would lead us to not to reject the hypothesis that income has no effect on the quantity demanded. **In short the minimum variance does not imply that the numerical value of the variance will be small.**
4. Multicollinearity is a sample phenomenon in the sense that even if the X variables are not linearly related in the population, they can be related in a particular sample. when we postulate the PRF we assume that all the X variables in the model have a separate or independent effect on the dependent variable Y. But it can also happen that the variables are so highly collinear that we can not isolate their individual influence on Y. And this happens because most economic data are not obtained in a controlled laboratory experiments, if these data could be obtained experimentally to begin with, we would not allow collinearity to exist.
