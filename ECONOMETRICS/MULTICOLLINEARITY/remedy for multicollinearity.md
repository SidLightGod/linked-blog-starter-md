1. dropping a variable from the model : faced with multicollinearity the simplest solution might seem to be to simply drop a variable from the model. However dropping a variable may lead to specification bias. By dropping a variable we might be obtaining biased estimates of the coefficients for the retained variables in the model.
2. acquiring additional data or a new sample : since multicollinearity is a sample feature, it is possible that for another sample with the same variables, collinearity may not be serious as in the first sample. the important concern is that whether obtaining another sample is feasible or not because collection of data can be costly. sometimes just increasing the sample size may also reduce the severity of the collinearity problem. 
3. rethinking the model : sometimes a model chosen for empirical analysis is not carefully thought out, maybe some important variables are omitted, or maybe the functional form of the model is incorrectly chosen. It is possible that in the LIV specification the extent of collinearity may not be as high as in the log linear specification or some other.

5. transformation of the variables : occasionally transformation of the variables in the model can minimize, if not solve, the problem of collinearity.
