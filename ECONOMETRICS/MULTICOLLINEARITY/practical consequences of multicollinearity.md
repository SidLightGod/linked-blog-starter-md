1. large variances and standard errors of OLS estimators. If the standard error of the coefficient increases, it becomes more difficult to estimate the true value of the estimator, that is there is fall in the precision in the OLS estimator. 
2. wider confidence interval : because of large standard errors, the population interval for the relevant population parameters tend to be large. 
3. insignificant t ratios : to test the hypothesis we compare the t statistic with the t critical value but in the case of multicollinearity the standard errors increase this in turn dramatically decreases the t statistic value which implies that there would be a greater likelihood of us failing to reject the null hypothesis that the true population coefficient is zero.
4. a high $R^2$ but very few significant t ratios 
5. OLS estimators and their standard errors become very sensitive to small changes in the data, that is, they tend to be unstable. This is so because in case of multicollinearity, the explanatory variables have correlation among them so it becomes hard to isolate their individual impact, also in such case the standard errors increase and higher variance, making the model unstable and very sensitive to changes in the data. 
6. wrong signs for regression coefficients. In case of multicollinearity, the coefficients may imply an oppositive relationship between the explanatory variable and the dependent variable than the a priori expectation. for example in case of income and price on demand, if price and income are collinear, the income may imply a negative relationship with demand and price may imply a positive relationship with demand. 
7. difficulty in assessing the individual contributions of the explanatory variables to the explained sum of squares ESS or $r^2$. This happens because of the collinearity among the explanatory, this leads to one of the variables to soak up explanatory power of the other.
