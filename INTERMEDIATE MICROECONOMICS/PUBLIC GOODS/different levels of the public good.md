1. suppose the two roommates have to decide how much money to spend on the TV. The more money they spend the better TV they can get. 
2. we will let $x_1$ and $x_2$ measure the private consumption of each person and $g_1$ and $g_2$ be their contributions to the TV. 
3. let G measure the quality of the TV they buy and let the cost function for the quality be given by c(G). This means that if two roommates want to purchase a TV of quality G, they have to spend c(G) dollars to do. 
4. the constraints facing the roommates is that the total amount they spend on their public good and private consumption has to add to how much money they have : $$ x_1 + x_2 + c(G) = w_1 + w_2 $$

8. given the marginal rate of substitution we know that the person 1 would be willing to accept 1/4 more dollars of the private good for the loss of 1 dollar of the public good. 
9. similarly person 2 would accept 1/2 more dollars of the private good for a dollar decrease in the public good. suppose we reduce the amount of public good and offer to compensate both the individuals. when we reduce the public good by one unit we save a dollar. 
10. after we pay each individual the amount of dollars they need to allow this change (3/4 = 1/4 + 1/2), we find that we still have 1/4 of a dollar left over. And this money could be shared between 2 individuals, thereby making them both better off. 
11. similarly, if the sum of marginal rates of substitutions were greater than 1, we could increase the amount of the public good to make them both better off. 
12. if the $|MRS_1| = 2/3$ and $|MRS_2| = 1/2$, say, this means that person 1 gives up 2/3 of a dollar of private consumption to get one more unit of the public good and person 2 would give up 1/2 of a dollar of private consumption to get 1 unit more of the public good. 
13. but if person 1 gave up his 2/3 units and person 2 gave up his 1/2 unit, we would have enough to produce the extra unit public good, since the marginal cost of providing the public good is 1. 
14. thus we can give the left over amount back to the people, thereby making them both better off
15. one way to interpret this is to imagine the marginal rate of substitution as the marginal willingness to pay for an extra unit of the public good. 
16. then the efficiency condition just says that the marginal willingness to pay must equal the marginal cost of providing an extra unit of the public good.
