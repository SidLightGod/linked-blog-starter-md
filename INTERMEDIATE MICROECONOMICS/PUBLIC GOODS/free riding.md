1. free riding is similar but not identical to the prisoner's dilemma. 
2. suppose that each person has a wealth of $500 and each person values the TV at $100 and that the cost of the TV is $150. 
3. since the sum of the reservation prices is greater than the cost of the TV it is pareto efficient to buy the TV. 
4. let us suppose that there is no way to exclude the other person from watching the TV and that each roommate will decide independently whether or not to buy the TV. 
5. Consider the decision of one of the roommates, player A. If he buys the TV, he gets a benefit of $100 and pays $150 dollars, leaving him with a net benefit of $-50, however if A buys the TV, B gets to watch it for free and gets a net benefit of $100.

7. the dominant strategy in this game is for neither player to buy the TV. If player A decides to buy the TV, it is in the best interest of B to free ride. 
8. if player A decides not to buy then it is in the interest of B to not buy the TV either. 
9. this is similar to prisoner's dilemma but not exactly the same as it. The strategy that maximizes the sum of player utilities is for each player to make the same choice. 
10. if player A buys the TV and both players watch it, we can simply construct a pareto improvement simply by having player B make a side payment to player A. 
11. if player A gives player B $51, then both the players will be better off when player A buys the TV. More generally any payment between 50 to 100 will result in pareto improvement for this example.
