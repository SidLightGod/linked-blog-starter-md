1. let us start with a simple example where we have 2 room mates 1 and 2. They are deciding whether to purchase a TV or not. 
2. Given the size of their apartment, the TV will go in the living room and will be watched by both the roommate. 
3. thus it will be a public good and not a private good. Lets denote w1 and w2 as the wealth of the two individuals and g1 and g2 as each person's contribution to the TV and x1 and x2 as the money left over to spend on each private consumption. 
4. the budget constraint are given by : $$ x_1 + g_1 = w_1 $$ $$ x_2 + g_2 = w_2 $$
5. we suppose that the TV costs C dollars. So to purchase it the two combinations must be $$ g_1 + g_2 \geq c $$
6. this summarizes the technology available to provide the public good : roommate can acquire the TV if they together pay the cost c. 
7. the utility of person 1 will depend on the private consumption of his or her private consumption x1 and the availability of the public good TV. 
8. we write person 1's utility function as $$ u_1(x_1, G) $$ where G will either be 0 or 1, 1 in the case of the availability of TV. 
9. to measure the value that each person puts on the public good TV, we can judge how much each of them is willing to pay for the TV, this is called the reservation price.
10. the reservation price is the maximum amount that person 1 would be willing to pay to have the TV present. 
11. this is the price r1, such that the person is just indifferent between paying r1 and having the TV available and not having to pay at all. 
12. if person 1 pays the reservation price r1, then the person has w1 - r1 available for the private consumption. 
13. if he does not get the TV, he will have w1 amount left for private consumption. 
14. if he is to be indifferent between these two alternatives then it must hold true that $$ u_1(w_1 - r_1, 1) = u_1(w_1, 0) $$
15. a similar equation can be used to explain the reservation price for person 2. an important thing to be noted is that in general the reservation of each person will depend on the wealth of that person. The maximum a person will be willing to pay depends on how much the person is able to pay.  
16. each individual's private consumption is determined by the wealth that is left over to them after making their contributions to the public good. 
17. **the condition under which the public good shall be provided is when there is payment scheme such that both the people will be better off having the TV and paying their share than not having the TV.**
18. this implies the following condition to be satisfied $$ u_1(w_1, 0) < u_1(x_1, 1) $$ $$  u_2(w_2, 0) < u_2(x_2, 1) $$
19. $$ w_1 - r_1 < w_1 - g_1 $$ and $$  w_2 - r_2 < w_2 - g_2 $$ so, $$ r_1 > g_1 $$ and, $$ r_2 > g_2 $$
20. this is a condition that must be satisfied if an allocation $(w_1, w_2, 0)$ is Pareto inefficient : it must be that the contribution each person is making for the TV is less than the willing to pay for the TV. 
21. if a person can acquire a good for less than the maximum that he would be willing to pay, then the acquisition would be to his benefit. 
22. thus the condition that the reservation price exceeds the cost share simply states that a Pareto improvement will result when each roommate can acquire the TV for a price less than the maximum that he would be willing to pay for it. This is clearly a necessary condition for the purchase of TV to be a pareto improvement. 
23. this is also implies that $$ r_1 + r_2 > g_1 + g_2 = c $$

###### few things to keep in mind 
1. first it is important to note that the condition describing when the provision of public good will be a pareto improvement only depend on each agent's willingness to pay and on the total cost. **If the sum of the reservation price exceeds the total cost of the TV, then there will exist a payment scheme such that both people will be better off having the public good than not having it.**
2. second, whether or not it is pareto efficient will depend on the initial distribution of wealth, this is true because in general the reservation prices will depend on the distribution of the wealth. 

# the case of quasilinear preferences 
1. suppose the preferences of the two roommates were quasi linear which implies the following $$ u_1(x_1, G) = x_1 + v_1(G) $$ and, $$  u_2(x_2, G) = x_2 + v_1(G) $$
2. where G will be 0 or 1, depending on whether or not the public good is available. 
3. suppose that v1(0) = v2(0) = 0, this implies that no TV provides 0 utility from watching the TV.
4. in this case the definitions of the reservation prices become : $$ u1(w_1 - r_1, 1) = w_1 - r_1 + v_1(1) = u_1(w_1, 0) = w_1 $$
5. this then implies that the reservation prices are given by $$ r_1 = v_1(1) $$ and, $$  r_2 = v_2(1) $$
6. thus the reservation price is independent of the amount of wealth and hence the optimal provision of the public good will be independent of the wealth, at least over some range of wealth.
