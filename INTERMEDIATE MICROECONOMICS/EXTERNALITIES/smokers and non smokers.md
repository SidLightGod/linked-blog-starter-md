1. we will imagine two roommates A and B, who have preference over "money" and "smoke"
2. we suppose that both like money but that A likes smoke and B likes clean air 
3. we can depict the consumption possibilities of the two individuals using the Edgeworth box. The length of the horizontal axis will represent the total amount of money the two agents have, and the height of the vertical axis will represent the total amount of smoke that can be generated. 
4. The preference of A is increasing both in the case of money and smoke, while B's preferences are increasing in money and clean air - the absence of smoke. 
5. we will measure the smoke on a scale on a scale of 0 to 1, where 0 implies no smoke and 1 implies a smoke filled room.

7. The amount of smoke is a good for A and a bad for B. so that B moves to a more preferred position as A consumes less smoke. B is made better off when A reduces his consumption of smoke. 
8. the initial endowment of the smoke depends on the legal system. 

# situation where B has the right to a clean air 
1. in this situation the initial endowment must be at the point E where the money is 100 dollars but the amount of smoke is 0. 
2. one of the aspect of having a property right to clean air is having the right to trade it for something else valuable for example money. It can be the case that B wants to trade some of the clean air for more money
3. as before the Pareto efficient allocation is the one where neither consumer can be made better off without the other being made worse off. 
4. the point of tangency where the marginal rates of substitution between smoke and money should be the same between the two agents. It is easy to imagine A and B trading to such a pareto efficient point, in this case the point X.

# situation where A has the right to smoke as much as he wants to 
1. in this situation the initial endowment point would be at E', this is however, again not a pareto efficient point. 
2. there can be a mutually beneficial trade to a point X'. 


> so X and X' are both the pareto efficient points however we arrive at them from different initial endowment points. 


# the problem of not well defined property rights 
1. If A believes that he has the right to smoke and B believes that he has a right to clean air, then we have a problem at our hands. 
2. the practical problems of externalities arise because of poorly defined property rights. 
3. cases where the property rights are not well defined leads to an inefficient production of externalities, which means there would be ways to make both parties involved better off by changing the production of externalities. 
4. If the property rights are well defined and mechanisms are in place to allow for negotiations between people, then people can trade their rights to produce externalities in some that they trade rights to produce and consume ordinary goods.
