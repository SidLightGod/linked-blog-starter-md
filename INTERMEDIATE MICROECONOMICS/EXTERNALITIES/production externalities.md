Let us now consider the situation involving production externalities. 

1. Firm S produces some amount of steel, s, and also produces a certain amount of pollution, x, which it dumps into a river. 
2. Firm F, a fisher, is located downstream and is adversely affected by S's pollution. 
3. suppose that firm S's cost function is given by $$C_s (s,x) $$ where s is the amount of steel produced and x is the amount of pollution produced. 
4. Firm F's cost function is given by the $$ C_f (s,x) $$ where f indicates the production of fish and x the amount of pollution. 
5. Note that F's cost of producing a given amount of fish depends on the amount of pollution produced by the steel firm. 
6. We will suppose that the pollution increases the cost of providing fish $$ \frac{\delta{C_f} }{\delta{x}} > 0 $$ and that pollution deceases the cost of steel production, 
7. $$  \frac{\delta{C_f} }{\delta{x}} \leq 0 $$ this last assumption says that increasing the amount of pollution decreases the cost of producing steel.

9. Note that the steel mill gets to choose the amount of pollution it wants to generate, but the fishery takes the level of pollution as outside of its control
10. ![[Pasted image 20250419125821.png]]
11. These conditions say that at the profit maximization point, the price of each good - steel and pollution should be equal to its marginal cost. 
12. In the case of steel firm, one of its product is pollution which by assumption has a zero price. 
13. so the profit maximization supply of pollution condition says to produce pollution until the cost of an extra unit is zero. 
14. the externality here is not hard to see: fishery cares about the production of pollution meanwhile the steel firm, when it makes the profit maximization calculation does not take into consideration the cost it imposes on the fishery while production of steel, this social cost is ignored by the steel firm. 

###### Pareto Efficient production plan for steel and fish? 
1. suppose the fishery and steel firm merged to form a single firm that produced both fish and steel (and possibly pollution). 
2. Then there would be no externality, as externality is generated when one firm's action affects another firm's production possibilities. 
3. if there was only a single firm it would take into account the interaction between the different divisions of the firm while deciding the profit maximization production plan. 
4. we say that the **externality has been internalized** by this reassignment of the property rights.
