1. a social welfare function is just some function of individual utility functions : $$ W( u_1(x),.....,u_n(x) ) $$
2. it gives a way to rank different allocation that depends only one individual preferences, and it is an increasing function of each individual's utility 
3. one special case mentioned in the above is the sum of the individual utility functions $$ W(u_1,...., u_n) = \sum_{i = 1}^n u_i $$
4. this is sometimes called the classical utilitarian or Benthamite welfare function. A slight generalization of this form is the weighted - sum - of - utilities welfare function. $$  W(u_1,...., u_n) = \sum_{i = 1}^n a_iu_i $$
5. here the weights a1, a2, ....an are supposed to be numbers indicating how important each agent's utility is to the overall social welfare. It is natural to take ai, as being positive. 
6. another interesting welfare function is the minmax or Rawlsian social welfare function : $$   W(u_1,...., u_n) = min(u_1,......u_n) $$
7. this welfare function says that the social welfare of an allocation depends only on the welfare of the worst off agent - the person with the minimal utility.
