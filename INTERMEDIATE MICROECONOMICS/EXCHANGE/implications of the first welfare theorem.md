1. the first welfare theorem states that the competitive equilibrium is pareto efficient. 

###### some implicit assumptions of this theorem 
1. one assumption is that the agents only care about their own consumption of the goods, and not about what others consume. 
2. if one agent does care about other agent's consumption we say that there exists a consumption externality. 
3. another important implicit assumption in the first welfare theorem is that the agents actually behave competitive. 
4. finally the first welfare theorem is only of interest if a competitive equilibrium actually exist 
5. given these provisions, the first welfare theorem is a pretty strong result, a private market with each agent seeking to maximize their utility will result in an allocation that achieves pareto efficiency. 

###### importance of the first welfare theorem
1. the importance of the first welfare theorem is that it provides a general mechanism using which we can we can ensure Pareto efficient outcomes. 
2. this does not matter much if there exists only 2 people and examine the possibility of mutual trade together. 
3. but if there exists thousands or even millions of people then there must be some kind of structure imposed on the trading process. 
4. the first welfare theorem shows that the particular structure of competitive markets has desirable property of achieving a Pareto efficient allocation. 
5. the consumers do not need to know anything about how the goods are produced, or who owns the goods, or where the goods come from in a competitive market. 
6. if each consumer only knows the price of the good, he can determine his demands, and if the market functions well enough to determine the competitive prices, we are guaranteed an efficient outcome.
