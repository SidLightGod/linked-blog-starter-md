1. the second theorem of welfare economics asserts that under certain conditions, every pareto efficient allocation can be achieved as a competitive equilibrium. 
2. the second welfare theorem implies that the problems of distribution and efficiency can be separated. 
3. whatever pareto efficient allocation you want can be supported by the market mechanism. The market mechanism is distributionally neutral, whatever your criteria for a good or just a distribution of welfare, you can use competitive markets to achieve it. 
4. prices play two roles in the market system, an allocative role and a distributive role. 
5. the allocative role of the prices is to indicate relative scarcity. The distribution role is to determine how much of different goods different agents can purchase. 
6. the second welfare theorem suggests that these two roles can be separated. we can redistribute endowments of goods to determine how much wealth agents have, and then use prices to indicate relative scarcity. 
7. policy discussions often become confused on this point. one often hears arguments for intervening in the pricing decisions on grounds of redistribution-al equity. However, such redistribution is often misguided.
8. a convenient way of achieving efficient allocations is for each agent to face the true social costs of his or her actions and to make choices that reflect those costs.
9. thus in a perfectly competitive market, the marginal decision to consume more or less will depend on the price - which measures how everyone values the good on the margin. 
10. the considerations of efficiency are inherently marginal decisions. each person should face the correct marginal tradeoff in making his or her consumption decisions. 
11. the decision about how much different agents should consume is a totally different issue. in a competitive market this is determined by the value of the resources that a person has to sell. 
12. all that is necessary is the transfer of the purchasing power of the endowment. 
13. the state could tax one consumer on the basis of the value of his endowment and transfer this money to another. as long as the tax are based on the value of the endowment of the goods there will be no loss of efficiency. 
14. it is only in the case when the taxes depend on the choices that a consumer makes that inefficiencies result. 

###### the role of tax
1. it is true that tax on endowment would generally change the behavior of the people. but according to the first welfare theorem trade from any initial endowments will result in a Pareto efficient allocation. 
2. taxing the labor that people sell in the market is called a distortionary tax. if the sale of labor is taxed then the labor supply decision of the consumers will be distorted, they will likely supply less labor. 
3. taxing the endowment of the labor is however non distortionary. this is because the potential value of the labor is not something that is changed due to tax. 
4. suppose we consider a society where the labors would have to give money earned in 10 hours of his labor time to the state each week. This kind of tax will be independent of how much the labor worked and depend only on the endowment of the labor. 
5. according to the second welfare theorem such a tax would be non distortionary. essentially any pareto efficient allocation could be achieved by lump sum redistribution. 
6. the important implication of the second welfare theorem is that the price should be used to reflect scarcity. the lump sum wealth should be used to adjust for distributional goals. to a larger degree these two policies can be separated.
